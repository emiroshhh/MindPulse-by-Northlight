# MindPulse qualitative multilingual search-intent research

Research date: 2026-08-20

## Data boundary

No Google Search Console property or export was available in this task. No search-volume, keyword-difficulty, CTR, rank, or traffic estimate is reported. The findings below are **QUALITATIVE SEARCH-INTENT RESEARCH** based on current result-page composition, wording in result titles/snippets, product fit, and the content already present on MindPulse.

The two initial acquisition concepts are deliberately limited to:

1. AI study planner — clear tool/product intent and a direct match for Daily Planner.
2. Catching up on missed schoolwork — a distinct problem-solving intent and a direct match for Recovery Mode.

A separate “AI study assistant” page was not created. That phrase is relevant, but the current homepage already targets it; another broad product page would overlap the homepage and risk thin, repetitive content.

## English

Current results for “AI study planner” are dominated by product/tool pages that explain inputs, schedules, deadlines, and concrete workflows, plus comparison articles. Examples include [IntelliPlan](https://intelliplan.tech/), [Taskoo](https://taskoo.ca/), and [Taskade's comparison guide](https://www.taskade.com/blog/ai-study-planners). Results for overwhelmed or behind students shift toward informational guidance rather than product pages; the wording “I don't know where to begin” is also used in student-support material such as this [Grand Valley State University study guide](https://www.gvsu.edu/cms4/asset/91D2F15F-DAF4-C2F7-B659ECCACE9E7375/make_study_time_effective.pdf).

## Kazakh

Kazakh-language results were notably sparse for direct study-planner and missed-work phrases. Results more often discuss students using artificial intelligence, AI literacy, formal “оқу жоспары”, and general study routines. Examples include [QyzPU's AI Sana page](https://www.qyzpu.edu.kz/kz/ai-sana), an [Egemen Qazaqstan article on student AI use](https://egemen.kz/article/407586-zhasandy-intellektini-studentter-qalay-qoldanyp-dgur), and [Kazakhstan government guidance on students' daily routines](https://www.gov.kz/memleket/entities/departament-kkbtu-pavlodar/press/news/details/376459).

This sparse result set is not evidence of high or low demand. It is a reason to use clear, natural problem language and obtain native review before drawing conclusions.

## Content gaps intentionally deferred

- Comparison pages: MindPulse does not yet have enough stable, verified competitor and feature evidence to publish a fair comparison.
- Separate exam, homework, motivation, procrastination, and deadline pages: these would fragment the two stronger guides before Search Console shows distinct demand.
- Scaled translated pages, country/city variants, and synonym pages: rejected as doorway/thin-content risk.
